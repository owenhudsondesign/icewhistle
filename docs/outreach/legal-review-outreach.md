# Outreach: pro bono legal review of ICEwhistle content

Send individually, not as a group email. Fill every `[bracket]` before sending
— the blanks are things only you can answer accurately. Do not guess at
traffic numbers; if you would rather not share them, cut that sentence.

---

## Core email

**Subject:** Pro bono review of know-your-rights content — free immigration app, ~2 hours

Hello [Name],

I build and maintain ICEwhistle (https://icewhistle.app), a free
know-your-rights app for immigrant communities. It is a solo, unfunded
project: no login, no accounts, no user tracking, nothing sold. I am writing
to ask whether [organization] would consider reviewing its legal content for
accuracy.

**The ask is narrow.** I am not looking for representation, an ongoing
relationship, or advice for any individual. I want a qualified immigration
attorney to read roughly [N] screens of existing guidance and tell me what is
wrong, out of date, or missing. I would guess two to three hours. I will send
the content however is easiest — a document, a link, or a PDF.

**Why I am asking now.** The app tells people they have the right to remain
silent, the right to a lawyer before answering questions, not to sign anything
without one, and not to open the door without a judicial warrant. I recently
checked that guidance against current sources and found several areas where
the ground appears to have shifted — expedited removal, the status of
administrative warrants and home entry, and reported pressure on detained
people to sign voluntary departure. I am not qualified to judge any of it, and
I am not willing to publish guesses to people who may be relying on this
during an encounter. So the content is unchanged and the open questions are
written up separately, ready to hand to whoever is willing to look.

**What already exists,** so you know this is a working tool and not an idea:

- Guidance covering encounters at home, at work, in a vehicle, and after
  someone is detained
- A directory of 55 verified hotlines across 27 states, checked against each
  organisation's own published number
- Full offline support, so it works with no signal
- 30 languages, including right-to-left support for Arabic, Farsi and Urdu
- Built to WCAG AA, tested with screen readers in mind
- [Add a line about current usage if you are comfortable sharing it]

I am happy to credit [organization] in the app, or not to — whichever you
prefer. I am equally happy to be told the content needs more work than a
review can fix.

If this is not something you take on, I would be grateful for a pointer to
someone who might.

Thank you for your time.

[Your name]
[Email] · https://icewhistle.app

## Notes

- **Send one at a time**, roughly a week apart, in the ranked order above.
  Several of these are small teams and a visible mass mailing reads badly.
- **Law school clinics run on the academic calendar.** Late September is good
  timing for the autumn term; if you get no reply, try again in January.
- **Two to avoid for this specific ask:** ILRC states it does not provide
  individual consultations, and the Immigration Justice Campaign is scoped to
  direct case representation rather than content review.
- **Attach or link `docs/legal-content-review-2026-09-23.md`** once someone
  says yes. It is already organised as questions for an attorney, which makes
  their job much smaller and makes you look like a serious collaborator.
- **If someone says yes,** add a "last reviewed" date and their credit line to
  the rights pages. That date is worth as much to a reader as the review
  itself.
